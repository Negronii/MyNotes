# Writing Guide

This is the single source of writing rules for MyNotes. Notes are a durable knowledge base; interview review is one way to use them, not the limit of what belongs here.

## Organize knowledge

- A chapter answers one question at the scale of a complete mechanism or decision. Keep the steps a reader needs for that answer as sections of the same chapter. Give a mechanism its own chapter when it has an independent question and meaningful use in at least two different contexts. A map entry, glossary mention, or backlink to its parent does not count as a second use.
- Use the six domain roots as the top-level boundary: `ai-engineering`, `frontend`, `engineering-foundations`, `archive`, `review`, and `maintenance`. Engineering foundations holds shared networking, operating systems, databases, API reliability, security, testing, deployment, backend, and system-design knowledge. Create topic folders when substantive notes need them; authorized, source-backed additions may fill knowledge gaps.
- Keep this open-source repository to general, reusable knowledge. Exclude personal résumé or project-experience material, internal project, system, code, or organization details, and unpublished results or data.
- Use English kebab-case filenames without numeric prefixes for new pages. Use subdirectories where they clarify ownership. Domain maps own the complete reading order. The knowledge navigator offers shorter routes through shared questions.
- Keep original attachments, source files, and prompts intact as a classified package. Course Markdown is part of the knowledge review.

## Plan a reading route

- Put a few questions at the top of each active domain map and identify a good first chapter. Each route follows one concrete run, failure, or decision from an observable starting point to an outcome. Carry the same case across the steps and any chapters that continue it.
- At a chapter handoff, explain what the upstream step does, how it works at the level needed for the case, and what it receives and produces. The chapter that goes deeper starts from that known input or intermediate state. Stop each chapter at the outcome owned by its question.
- When changing a route, identify where readers first learn a mechanism, where they reuse it, and which material they can look up as needed.
- Before reorganizing multiple domains, try the proposed reading experience on one existing chapter and one path the current chapters do not connect. Use forced jumps and points where a reader loses the causal chain to decide where better navigation or more explanation is needed.

## Write for understanding and reuse

- Put the question in the title, and make the title and opening promise the scope the chapter actually explains. When a particular model, framework, version, or implementation is the main subject, name it at the entrance; when it illustrates a general mechanism, mark the transition to that example where it occurs. Keep this scope clear in independently readable figures and incoming link descriptions. When using a running example, introduce it as a case study and establish who is trying to do what, what information they have, and what outcome they need. Introduce identifiers and implementation details when they help explain a step. For a process, follow one concrete input through the transformations and show the relevant intermediate state, such as tensor shapes, protocol messages, or ownership changes. For a decision, connect the constraints to the choices and show how to tell when a choice fails.
- Make an end-to-end chapter readable without opening its links. For each handoff, state what the preceding step produced and why the next step needs it. Work backward from the answer to the prerequisites that explain it. Readers have studied undergraduate computing, mathematics, and statistics but may not recall them fluently. At a likely stumbling point, supply a short local refresher with a concrete example and explain what the operation or concept contributes to this step. Apply this to mathematical notation, probability, protocols, state, and other prerequisites as needed.
- Write chapter titles, headings, prose, figure explanations, and literature contributions primarily in natural Chinese. Retain the most commonly used English form of technical jargon. At its first meaningful occurrence within a chapter or literature collection, use `English (中文)` when there is an established or widely used Chinese translation, for example `attention head (注意力头)`. Use English alone thereafter, and from the outset when no suitable common Chinese translation exists. Keep ordinary connective and explanatory language in Chinese; do not translate whole paragraphs twice or replace ordinary Chinese words with unnecessary English. Introduce acronyms with their full term when needed. Preserve exact source titles, API names, code identifiers, and example inputs whose wording matters to the explanation. When changing a title or heading, update affected inbound links in the same change. Keep structural markers required by repository scripts, such as the reading-order heading, unchanged.
- Organize the explanation around the reader's next question. Establish the overall process before zooming into a mechanism, keep its detailed steps together under descriptive subheadings, and reconnect its output to the surrounding process.
- Build the answer through the chapter's explanation. Introduce terms where used and keep useful material from old template sections in the relevant explanation. Do not add template sections for short answers, key-term lists, or self-tests. Adapt the explanatory pattern to the chapter's own question. See the [AI service walkthrough](../ai-engineering/systems/from-message-to-answer.md) for a complete run and the [language model walkthrough](../ai-engineering/foundations/how-llms-work.md) for a closer look at one mechanism.
- Preserve useful knowledge and distinctive examples when rewriting, merging, or splitting notes. Remove genuine duplication, but do not delete lasting material only because it is not currently an interview topic.
- Explain a linked mechanism's role at the point where the reader needs it. Follow natural questions from mechanism to engineering choice, failure mode, and evaluation when relevant. Give enough context locally to explain why the next question matters, then use a descriptive question-based link to the canonical chapter. Link directly to the relevant section when the chapter covers a broader question. Apply the chapter-splitting rule before creating another page.
- Use Obsidian callouts for focused material at the point of use. Use `[!info]` for short definitions, local prerequisite reminders, and supplements. Use `[!example]` for worked cases or calculations. Reserve `[!warning]` for conditions that change the conclusion or common mistakes that lead to a wrong result. The default theme shows `info` in blue and `example` in purple. Themes can change colors, so identify each callout by its type and a descriptive title. Keep the main reasoning in the prose, leave examples needed to follow it expanded, and fold only optional detail.
- Use explanatory figures when they make a mechanism or comparison easier to understand. Follow [figure planning and acceptance](#plan-and-accept-explanatory-figures) to preserve the knowledge chain while dividing work between pictures and prose.
- Show examples readers can follow on the page, including what changes when a real condition changes. When switching to a smaller synthetic example, explain why and specify the synthetic inputs and assumptions, such as the vocabulary, parameters, and chosen values. Distinguish values calculated in the example from reproduced or measured results. Include runnable code when the code's behavior is itself part of the explanation or the reader asks to run it. Reserve quizzes and exercises for explicit requests.

## Plan and accept explanatory figures

The lead author owns the explanation's completeness and correctness. A reader's approval of the visual style, or a cold read that finds no problems, does not establish that all necessary knowledge is present. The author must check the mechanism against evidence before handing it to a reviewer.

### Decide what each figure explains

1. Establish the complete mechanism needed to answer the chapter's question before choosing panels. Trace the input, transformations or decisions, intermediate states, result, and relevant branches or stopping conditions. Reconcile the existing note with authoritative evidence; an earlier draft or model memory alone cannot establish coverage. Keep this planning proportional to the change, without adding a process document to every note.
2. Divide at meaningful learning boundaries: a change in representation, a switch from overview to detail, or a point where the reader needs an explanation before proceeding. Each figure answers a local question and hands a clearly identified result to the next figure or paragraph. Keep tightly coupled operations together; use several figures with intervening prose when that makes the causal path easier to follow. There is no fixed number of panels. Mark a detail view as an expansion of an existing step, so it does not look like an extra operation.
3. Choose the visual form for the relationship. Mermaid suits flows and interactions; precise vector diagrams suit layouts, tensors, and local transformations; tables and plots suit comparisons and quantities. Keep section titles and surrounding explanations in Markdown. Let figures show relationships and changes while prose explains purpose, prerequisites, and conditions. Use the same case and consistent notation throughout; identify any deliberate switch to a smaller example.

### Acceptance checklist

Use this checklist during editing; do not copy it into knowledge chapters.

- [ ] **The knowledge chain is intact.** Compare the final pictures and adjacent prose with the verified mechanism, not just with the old diagram. Every step needed to understand the answer has a home. A broad label such as “Decode” does not replace the score-to-probability-to-sampling explanation when that transformation is part of what the reader is learning. State consequential simplifications locally; retain their place in the causal chain.
- [ ] **Every handoff is identifiable.** The input and output of each operation are clear, including across pictures. Introduce symbols where needed; show relevant shapes, axes, units, or state changes. Distinguish parameters from runtime values and distinguish operations such as matrix multiplication, elementwise multiplication, addition, and concatenation. A formula identifies its operands; an operation on an arrow unambiguously applies to the value carried by that arrow.
- [ ] **The example works all the way through.** Trace the same input to the stated result. Recompute the intermediate values that explain the mechanism, and check dimensions, probabilities, units, or state transitions as applicable. Synthetic values and dimensions remain compatible across panels and are identified as teaching choices. Explain the outcome and any continuation or stop needed to finish the question.
- [ ] **Scope and alternatives are accurate.** Keep model-, version-, or implementation-specific conditions visible where they affect the conclusion. Do not depict one optional path as universal; for example, sampling uses a probability distribution while greedy selection can operate directly on logits. An overview and its detail views describe the same configuration.
- [ ] **The visual grammar is unambiguous.** Arrows have clear origins, destinations, directions, and visible shafts. A junction says whether values are added, concatenated, or merely routed. Labels and formulas sit by the objects they describe; color has a consistent meaning and is not the only way to identify a relationship. Technical names and subscripts are typeset correctly.
- [ ] **The actual reading size is comfortable.** Intuitive reading and legibility take precedence over footprint and information density. Allow space for the full rendered text, including subscripts and descenders, between formulas, dimension labels, shapes, and arrows. Expand the canvas or split the figure instead of shrinking text or crowding connections. Inspect the current asset in Obsidian at normal reading width, including its continuous-reading copy when present; ensure an image cache is not showing an older version.
- [ ] **The reviewed artifact is the delivered one.** Keep editable static assets in a nearby `resources` folder with relative Markdown links. Reconcile regenerated images with their prose and captions. Complete the applicable cold read and native checks from the maintenance workflow after resolving material findings; the lead author reviews the complete explanation and reports what was actually checked.

## Check factual claims

Model memory is a prompt to investigate, not evidence. For uncertain or consequential claims, check authoritative documentation, papers, or reproducible behavior. Distinguish an error from a version change, a conditional conclusion, and insufficient evidence. Do not describe an unverified claim as confirmed. Record a source, version, and verification date in the note when they matter to a key decision; omit them when they add no value.

State the conditions that make a mechanism hold. For version-sensitive APIs or examples, give the applicable version and identify the version actually run when verified. For an engineering recommendation, name what it optimizes, what it costs, and what condition would change the choice.

When a foundational paper informs current practice, read follow-up research and a current reference implementation. Teach the durable mechanism and the applicable current practice in one account. Keep older methods only where their assumptions explain the current design or help maintain an existing system. Keep study results scoped to the tested task, model, workload, and baseline.

## Keep sources available without interrupting reading

- Keep internal learning links beside the explanation they extend. Normally place external evidence in a single collapsed source callout at the chapter's end, using `> [!info]- 来源`. Each entry names the supported section or claim, the original URL, and the source version/date and section, page, or table where relevant. Preserve this mapping when moving inline citations; a list of URLs alone is insufficient.
- Keep qualifications needed to understand a claim in the visible prose, including model assumptions, experimental limits, and the distinction between a source's finding and an engineering extension. Keep an external link inline only when opening it is itself part of the current task, such as using a referenced API specification.
- Use the existing AI literature index as a curated collection grouped by topic. Each source has one entry with its contribution or reason to read, version/date, and canonical knowledge destinations. A chapter's source callout supports verification; the collection supports discovery. Do not create a separate source file for every chapter.

## Keep maintenance lightweight

- Obsidian is a reading interface; notes remain ordinary Markdown and should work outside Obsidian. For targeted native checks after link, path, or rendering changes, follow the [MyNotes maintenance workflow](../.agents/skills/mynotes-maintainer/SKILL.md).
- Do not add blanket YAML metadata, per-note learning or mastery status, or scheduled per-note review dates. When the user authorizes a central current-round checklist, it may record only read/not-read for that round; it is not a mastery claim or durable per-note status and must not be expanded into per-note YAML or scheduled reviews.
- `archive` means low-frequency or paused maintenance. It does not imply that content is wrong, obsolete, or deprecated.
- Domain `index.md` files are topic maps. Their one `## Reading order / 阅读顺序` section is the complete ordered list used to build continuous-reading documents. Keep each eligible article there once, with a working relative Markdown link. The map also handles navigation; do not create a second ordering source.
- Make changes in the source notes or domain maps and regenerate `review/generated/` through the [MyNotes maintenance workflow](../.agents/skills/mynotes-maintainer/SKILL.md#workflow).
